"Final years H. G. Wells in 1943
Wells's greatest literary output occurred before the First World War, which was lamented by younger authors whom he had influenced. In this connection, George Orwell described Wells as "too sane to understand the modern world", and "*since 1920 he has squandered his talents in slaying paper dragons."*[107] G. K. Chesterton quipped: "Mr Wells is a born storyteller who has sold his birthright for a pot of message".[108]
