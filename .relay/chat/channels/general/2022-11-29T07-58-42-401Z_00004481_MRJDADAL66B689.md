The Yonger Dryas is a brief period at the end of the 4.5 billion year Earth timeline in in the Uniformitarianism model. Contrasting with that theory is Catastrophic model, which asserts that the earth formed rapidly in a short series of 'creation' events over less than 6000 years. In the latter model, there is an ice age starting at 2193 B.C.E. when Earth leaves Saturn's last Absu layer. The polar regions experience a *permanent winter* since that moment. I don't mind using Boch Saga or the Yonger Dryas theory in a timeline, but the scale would have to be shortened considerably to be realistic. After all, there is no way to prove anything happened over thousands of years, if nobody was keeping a log of events every single day. Without that kind of source, we can't scale timelines that large. The only real evidence for million year timelines is the theory of carbon dating, which has been majorly called out as pseudoscience. There's no way to tell if a carbon atom decayed linearly over time, or decayed rapidly in a high-energy event.
